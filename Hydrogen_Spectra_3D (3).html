What the model shows · scientific notes

These apparatus views demonstrate emission and absorption spectra explained by Bohr’s quantised energy levels. They are teaching reconstructions, not replicas of apparatus personally used by Bohr. The four prominent visible hydrogen Balmer lines shown are approximately 410.2, 434.0, 486.1 and 656.3 nm. Emission involves transitions from n = 6, 5, 4 and 3 to n = 2; the corresponding absorption transitions go from n = 2 to those higher levels.

The absorption cloud represents atomic hydrogen with a population of atoms already in n = 2, as in suitably excited or warm gas. This is necessary for visible Balmer absorption. A cold cloud with nearly all atoms in n = 1 primarily absorbs Lyman-series ultraviolet radiation. Absorbed photons can be re-emitted in other directions, leaving reduced intensity along the transmitted viewing beam. The absorption view shows idealised dark lines for clarity.

The collimator helps form distinct slit images on the screen. Dispersion, apparatus dimensions, intensities, line widths, particle motion and flight times are illustrative. Shorter wavelengths are deflected more than longer wavelengths. The close-up uses a linear wavelength scale; the apparatus uses an illustrative dispersion mapping. Hydrogen also emits outside the visible region, and additional weaker Balmer lines are omitted.

References: NIST hydrogen spectral lines; University of Nebraska–Lincoln: hydrogen transitions; Laboratory measurements of hydrogen Balmer lines and n = 2 populations.
