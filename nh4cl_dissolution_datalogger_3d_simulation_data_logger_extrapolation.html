Press Start Logger to begin recording temperature against time, then Add NH₄Cl once you have a steady baseline — crystals drop into the water and dissolve completely. Because dissolving NH₄Cl is endothermic, the temperature falls as the solution draws heat from itself to break apart the ionic lattice. After it bottoms out, the cup (not being a perfect insulator) gains heat back from the surroundings and the temperature drifts back up in a roughly straight line. Press Stop Logger once you have enough of that warming trend, then Extrapolate Warming Curve to fit a straight line back through that data and read off the temperature it predicts at the moment of mixing — the standard way of correcting for heat exchange with the surroundings in a coffee cup calorimetry experiment.
