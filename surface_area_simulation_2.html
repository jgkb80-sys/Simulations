Surface Area — Calcium Carbonate + Hydrochloric Acid

CaCO3(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CaCl2(aq) + H2O(l) + CO2(g). A different reaction from the esterification simulations — a classic solid + liquid example, since surface area only matters when one reactant is a solid. HCl is in large excess here, so its concentration stays effectively constant.

Not every collision reacts — only ones with enough energy behind them (collision theory's other requirement, alongside surface area). An effective collision releases a CO2 bubble; that HCl molecule fades away and a fresh one appears elsewhere a moment later, so [HCl] stays constant while still giving you time to see each reaction happen. As you increase the number of pieces, the reference panel confirms the total CaCO3 hasn't changed — only its surface area and spacing has — so far more collisions (and reactions) happen per second. This is exactly the reasoning used for "powdered marble reacts faster than marble chips."
