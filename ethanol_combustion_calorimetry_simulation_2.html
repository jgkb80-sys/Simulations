The spirit burner is weighed before and after burning — the mass lost is the mass of ethanol combusted. The temperature rise of the known volume of water, together with that mass loss, is what lets the energy released per gram (and per mole) of ethanol be calculated afterwards. Heat losses to the surroundings and to the copper can itself are ignored here, which is why the enthalpy change calculated from a simple set-up like this is always noticeably less exergonic than the true (data book) value.
