50.0 cm³ of distilled water sits in the insulating polystyrene cup and its temperature is recorded first. Solid ammonium chloride powder is then added and, stirred continuously by the magnetic stirrer, fully dissolves. Because dissolving NH₄Cl is endothermic, the solution draws heat from itself to break apart the ionic lattice, and the temperature falls. That temperature drop, combined with the known moles dissolved, is what lets the molar enthalpy of solution be calculated afterwards. Heat gained back from the surroundings is ignored here, which is why a simple set-up like this gives a value somewhat smaller in magnitude than the true (data book) value of about +14.7 kJ mol⁻¹.
