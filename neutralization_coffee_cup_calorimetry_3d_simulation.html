The sodium hydroxide sits in the insulating polystyrene cup and its temperature is recorded first. The hydrochloric acid is then added, and the magnetic stirrer keeps the mixture uniform as the two neutralize. The temperature rise, combined with the known moles of acid and base reacted, is what lets the molar enthalpy of neutralization be calculated afterwards. Heat losses to the cup and surroundings are ignored here, which is why a simple set-up like this gives a value somewhat less exergonic than the true (data book) value of about −57.3 kJ mol⁻¹.
